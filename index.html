Engineering Questions

Technical insights into high-power battery performance, measurement, and isolation.

How does battery SOH affect system performance?
  • Under factory-ideal conditions, cell performance is nearly identical, allowing the pack to achieve its maximum nominal charge capacity.
  • Over continuous charge and discharge cycles, ongoing chemical degradation reduces total energy retention.
  • When a fully charged battery can only store 70% of its original maximum capacity, its State of Health (SOH) is defined as 70%.
  • While chemical aging occurs naturally, high-C-rate operations, mechanical vibration, and internal thermal gradients significantly accelerate this decay.
  • Because operating dynamic voltage differentials reflect the instantaneous degradation rate during operation, SOH is evaluated as a dynamic, real-time value.
How is battery SOH measured dynamically?
  • By continuously monitoring individual cell and full-pack parameters in real time and comparing them against baseline factory profiles, the system derives accurate, dynamic SOH values.
  • Real-time SOH enables reliable prediction of deliverable output energy and remaining range, preventing unexpected power cutoffs during high-load operations.
How do we design for high-power operation?
  • The chargeable capacity of a battery equals its deliverable output energy plus internal conversion losses (as electrical energy converts to chemical energy during charging, and chemical energy converts back to electrical energy during discharge).
  • As cell-to-cell consistency degrades, individual cell parameters diverge under varying operating conditions, rendering overall battery-level parameters incapable of accurately reflecting true remaining capacity.
  • In high-power discharge scenarios, the cell with the lowest operational voltage quickly triggers the discharge cutoff threshold during 3C–5C discharge rates. This activates automatic battery protection, causing sudden power cutoffs.
  • Conversely, during high-rate charging (1C), the cell with the highest voltage surge rapidly hits the overvoltage threshold, triggering charge protection and shutting down charging prematurely.
  • Deploying high-current active equalization serves as an essential solution to overcome these challenges in high-power applications—particularly for demanding mobility platforms such as eVTOL, eFoil, and eMotorcycle systems.
How do we design for waterproofing and corrosion resistance?
  • Utilizing IP68-rated sealed aluminum enclosures paired with anti-corrosion marine-grade anodization.
  • Applying isolated internal potting techniques designed to withstand harsh marine and dynamic outdoor environments.
  • In harsh marine environments, battery enclosures, connectors, and seal interfaces face extreme engineering challenges, including advanced waterproofing materials, mechanical vibration damping, and flexible internal electrical architecture.
